# Writing and demo copy

Use Dan's writing voice across the README, demo site, listing, setup flow,
screenshots, captions, release notes, and video script. Where the personal
Dan Voice skill is available, apply it alongside technical-writing-style for
technical material. Contributors can use the guidance below without those tools.

## Say what the person can do

Start with a real job: find the approved proposal, compare two contracts, save a
brief, or notice a change. Show the result before explaining the plumbing.
Use short paragraphs, concrete nouns, and words someone would say out loud.
Cut generic claims, forced jokes, manufactured enthusiasm, and em dashes.

Dan's first-person story is simple: he worked at Box and Meta, cares about both,
and wanted to build this. Do not invent anecdotes, company approval, or an
endorsement. Keep community ownership visible.

## Make every claim earn its place

Keep proposed, implemented, tested, and released capabilities distinct. A source
link supports a claim; it does not prove our connector works. Explain permissions
and limitations where they affect the user's decision. Never describe a draft
feature as available because it appears in a mockup or roadmap.

Keep the full research catalog in reach, but lead the demo site with a small
number of working jobs and the evidence behind them.

## Demo site copy direction

Proposed headline, for use once the core workflow is verified:

> Put your Box files to work with Muse.

Proposed supporting copy, subject to the same gate:

> Find the right document, answer with sources, and save the result back to Box.
> Built with love by Dan O'Leary, a Box and Meta alumnus.

Until an installable release exists, use “Follow the build” and “Explore the use
cases” as actions. Add installation language only when the instructions work.
Keep the independent community-project disclosure nearby.

## Screenshots and video

Capture the real Muse workflow once it passes. Show the question, source,
answer, and saved file. Use fictional documents. Keep private accounts and secrets
out of the frame. Label time cuts instead of implying an edited demo is real-time.

Narration should explain the decision that matters: why this proposal is the
approved one, what the conflicting draft says, or where the brief was saved.
Acknowledge missing evidence directly. Give viewers a useful next action.
